AV Interview Study v2

Visual-first prep for an AV foundation-models interview loop: two system-design rounds, one behavioral, and a possible pure-Python coding round. Every page teaches with interactive diagrams first, prose second.

Pages

Classification

Interactive decision-boundary and threshold widgets: drag the operating point along a live ROC/PR curve and watch precision, recall, and the confusion matrix update in real time.

Data→Inference

Follow a model from 10k labels to fleet scale with widgets for scaling curves, the imbalance/long-tail regime, and a latency-vs-throughput simulator for deployment tradeoffs.

Encoder

Design the multimodal perception encoder round visually: a clickable architecture diagram of camera/LiDAR/radar branches into BEV, with widgets comparing fusion points and token budgets.

Playbook

Interview run-of-show: interactive answer skeletons for both system-design rounds plus behavioral story cards, with timers and structure checklists you can rehearse against.

Math

The equations that come up, each paired with a manipulable plot: cross-entropy vs margin, bias-variance sliders, softmax temperature, and gradient fields you can hover.

GD Lab

A live gradient-descent sandbox on quadratics: tune the learning rate and condition number, watch iterates converge or diverge, then write the pure-Python version yourself.

Glossary

The centerpiece reference: a clickable concept map of ~35 core terms in five clusters, wired to 100+ interview-precise definitions with live search.

Four-week study order

  1. Week 1 — Foundations. Classification + Math. Get the fundamentals round cold: losses, metrics, bias-variance, calibration.
  2. Week 2 — Scale and speed. Data→Inference + GD Lab. Data complexity through deployment, and the possible pure-Python gradient-descent coding round.
  3. Week 3 — The design round. Encoder. Build and rehearse the multimodal perception encoder design end to end.
  4. Week 4 — Integration. Playbook + Glossary + mock interviews. Drill the concept map until every edge is explainable in one sentence.
